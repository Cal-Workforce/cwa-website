Input to the Dislocated Worker Journey Map

Who reaches Title I after a layoff

EDD's Workforce Services Branch is mapping the journey of a laid-off Californian with BCG. CWA offers the data on this page as one input to that work. It describes the laid-off workers California's 45 local workforce areas serve through WIOA Title I, the two programs they enroll in, and how that picture changes from one local area to the next.

8,144 were enrolled as Dislocated Workers and 7,317 as Adults. Their work histories before enrolling look much the same, so this page treats them as one dislocated worker customer served through two funding streams.

Local areas divide the same kind of customer between the two programs very differently. The split follows local funding and enrollment decisions more closely than local unemployment or wages. Each area's profile is below.

What the two halves share

Both lost recent, steady work and were unemployed when they enrolled. They trained at the same rate. Most of those enrolled as DW (63%) were also low income, so income does little to separate the two programs.

Where they differ

Those enrolled as DW are about eight years older, twice as likely to hold a degree, and earned about half again as much before enrolling. Both came mostly from service industries. Those enrolled as Adults came more often from health care and food service, and those enrolled as DW more often from manufacturing and professional services. In CalJOBS, about 80% of Dislocated Workers were UI claimants or exhaustees, compared with about 10% of Adult participants.

Outcome rows use people who exited from July 2023 to June 2024, the most recent group with wage records two quarters after exit. They describe the groups and do not measure the effect of services. Adult enrollees with a layoff pattern regained more than their prior earnings; one likely reason is that they started from lower wages.

Building on Dislocated Worker and RESEA growth

The growing funding streams already touch many of these workers

Dislocated Worker and RESEA funding are both growing, and EDD aims to make full use of both. Two parts of the data speak to where added DW enrollment and RESEA connections could come from.

Laid-off workers already enrolled as Adults

About 7,300 people a year enter Title I as Adults with the same kind of recent, steady work history as Dislocated Workers. Dislocated Worker eligibility rests on a layoff plus UI eligibility or attachment to the workforce, and a finding, under the local area's own definition, that a return to the same work is unlikely. Wage records can show the attachment even when no UI claim was filed.

For workers who qualify for both programs, either enrollment is allowable. With DW funds growing faster than Adult funds, DW enrollment gives local areas more room in both budgets.

RESEA as a connection into Title I

Under EDD's eligibility guide, a RESEA appointment carries Dislocated Worker eligibility with it. The Governor has determined that California's UI profiling and RESEA referral process meets the DW criteria in WIOA, so a claimant with a RESEA appointment needs no further documentation that they are unlikely to return to their prior work, and the UI claim itself shows the layoff and UI eligibility.

That makes the 179,144 claimants scheduled for a first RESEA from July 2025 to June 2026 ready candidates for DW enrollment. In the year ending June 2025, 88% of completed RESEAs led to reemployment services, and the state reported 124 claimants going on to training. The following year it reported 167.

The federal RESEA report does not record Title I enrollment, so the number of RESEA participants who go on to individualized career or training services, and through which program, is not yet visible. Measuring that step would show how much of the RESEA growth reaches Title I.

How the two connect. RESEA reaches the claimant side of the dislocated worker customer. The Adult enrollees with a layoff pattern are mostly the non-claimant side: in CalJOBS, about 10% of Adult participants were UI claimants.

  • Education points the same way

    Among dislocated worker customers, the share enrolled as DW rises from 44% of those with a high school diploma or less to 72% of those with a bachelor's degree, and the education difference holds within every age group.

  • Dislocated Workers mirror UI claimants by age

    Workers 45 and older were 45% of DW enrollees and 43% of California UI claimants over the same year. Of those enrolled as Adults with a layoff pattern, 27% were 45 or older and 20% were under 25, compared with 7% of claimants.

  • Eligibility rules lean the same direction

    DW eligibility draws on UI claims and work history, which build with age. Adult priority of service draws on low income, and older Dislocated Workers were less often low income: 55% of those 55 to 64, compared with 71% of those under 25.

  • Long-term unemployment rises with age among Adults

    Of Adult enrollees 45 and older, 37% to 39% had been unemployed 27 weeks or more, compared with 29% of those under 35.

What remains open. These records show who enrolled, not who could have. Whether older and more educated workers tend to engage only after a layoff would take a count of everyone who could benefit, such as California Policy Lab's linked UI and WIOA records.

Local areas

Forty-five local areas, each with its own pattern

A statewide average blends places that have little in common. Each local area makes its own reasonable choices about how to enroll and fund services for laid-off workers. The profiles below show those choices alongside who is served and the local economy, with no rankings.

What shapes the split

Local directors at the November 2 review raised several of these points. The data bear them out where they can.

  • Funding decisions made years ago

    Many local areas chose, under state guidance of the time, to enroll broadly as Adults and transfer DW funds for flexibility (see the history below). Fourteen areas transferred a combined $11.2 million from DW to Adult in program year 2024-25. In those areas 39% of dislocated worker customers were enrolled as DW, compared with 57% in the other 30. The enrollment pattern and the transfer go together.

  • Documenting eligibility

    DW enrollment asks staff to document the layoff and either UI eligibility or attachment to the workforce. For someone who never filed a UI claim, often a lower-wage service worker, Adult enrollment can be the quicker path to getting them help.

  • When people walk in

    Severance and savings can delay first contact by months, and when people do arrive their circumstances may have changed. One local area already tracks "days from layoff" as a measure.

  • Partners and geography

    Multi-county consortia and city boards that share a labor market with a county board work in different conditions. Networks of community partners also differ, and they shape who arrives and through which door.

  • Local economies explain less than expected

    Across areas, the share enrolled as DW shows almost no relationship to the 2025 unemployment rate or to average weekly wages. Local wages do track what Dislocated Workers earned before enrolling.

  • Each profile shows the local picture

    With DW allocations up in every area, each profile below shows that area's split, the people on each side of it, its funding history and its labor market.

Where today's pattern comes from

From 2006 to 2012, California's Integrated Service Delivery initiative, developed jointly by EDD and CWA with local directors, set out to serve WIA and Wagner-Peyser customers as one pool and to enroll every job seeker. State policy made that workable in ways that help explain enrollment patterns today.

WhenState action
2006 to 2009Federal waivers raised the share of DW funds a local area could move to Adult from 20% to 50%, then to 100% for program years 2007-08 and 2008-09.
July 2008In twelve pilot areas, every assessed adult was reported as a WIA Adult. Adult-only exiters there who were UI claimants rose from 527 to 7,732 in the first year.
August 2008Directive WSD08-2 let a local board that found Adult funds "not limited" serve adults regardless of income.
2011 to 2012The state evaluation recommended that all local areas enroll all clients, and EDD's 2012-2015 strategic plan adopted it. The transfer cap returned to 50%.

Broad Adult enrollment was a deliberate state approach at the time, and many areas carried it forward. That history helps explain why the split differs so much from place to place as DW funding grows.

How people move through it

People make their own way through services

A journey map has to draw a path. The people on it decide when to arrive, what to take, when to step away and whether to come back. Local directors raised this at the November 2 review, and the records bear it out, within the limits of what they can see.

  • Some come back

    6% of those enrolled as DW and 8% of those enrolled as Adults with a layoff pattern had another Title I enrollment in the public file. The file holds only recent records, so the share of people who return over time is higher.

  • The rules already expect it

    WIOA records a participant as exited only after 90 days with no services and none planned, dated back to the last service. In the data, every journey ends with a pause the system recognizes in hindsight.

  • Many do arrive close to the layoff

    69% of those enrolled as DW enrolled within six months of the layoff, and about a third of all dislocated worker customers stayed six months or more. A typical path exists for many people. The spread around it is wide.

  • What the records cannot show

    The public file carries the first and last service dates, so the stops in between are invisible. So are AJCC visits that never became an enrollment and the reasons people stop coming.

How people feel

Emotional modes, from the 2013 DOL and IDEO workshop

In April 2013, U.S. DOL ETA and IDEO ran a two-day design workshop on serving displaced workers with teams from California and Washington. IDEO's research found that job seekers arrive in different emotional modes, each with its own core need, and may shift between them in no set order. The framework starts from the person: the mode they are in and the services that help them in it. That keeps a system from treating people as groups who all move through it one way or respond to it the same way.

Any mode, at any point

A person can come in Panicked the week of a layoff or Elated about a lead months later. The same person can be Stalled one month and ready for focused action the next. A mode does not belong to a stage of the journey.

Same circumstances, different needs

Two laid-off workers with the same age, wage history and program can need different help because they are in different modes. The framework asks what this person needs now.

What the records can and cannot say

Participant records describe circumstances such as wages, income and UI status. They cannot show a mode. A conversation with staff, or the self-assessment the workshop proposed so job seekers can name the support they want, is where it becomes visible.

Connecting to the journey map

Where these data could attach

The draft journey map follows a laid-off worker from first hearing about services through exit. People enter at different points, skip stages, and leave and return, so each stage below is a place people pass through on their own timing. These are the points where the data on this page could add detail, offered for the team to use as it finds helpful.

Before first contact

  • RESEA is the largest existing contact with laid-off claimants, and a RESEA appointment already establishes DW eligibility.
  • Many in the half of the customer enrolled as Adults reach Title I without a UI claim: about 10% of Adult participants in CalJOBS were claimants.
  • Severance and savings stretch the time before people come in.

Front door and intake

  • The choice between Adult and DW enrollment is a real step in the journey, and it varies widely by local area.
  • UI status is the sharpest divider between the programs in CalJOBS. Wage records show recent steady work for many people who never filed a claim.

Planning and navigation

  • The two halves of the customer differ in age, education and prior earnings, while sharing a recent layoff.
  • A person can be in any emotional mode at any stage, so the help that fits can change from one visit to the next.

Training and services

  • Both halves trained at the same rate. Trainees enrolled as Adults used on-the-job training three times as often (17% compared with 5%), a sign that work-based options matter for lower-wage laid-off workers.

Exit and after

  • Wage records show employment and earnings for people who stop responding before a formal exit.
  • Federal rules record an exit only after 90 days without services, so the end of every journey is seen in hindsight.
  • The full dislocated worker customer can be counted from wage fields already in PIRL.
Additional data

Data that could add to the picture

EDD and research partners hold data that could round out these findings. Any of the following would help, as time and capacity allow.

  • RESEA participants who continue into Title I

    The share who go on to individualized career or training services, and whether they enroll as Adult or DW, statewide and by local area.

  • A profile of RESEA participants

    The federal RESEA reports carry counts only. A summary by age, education, prior earnings, prior industry and income would place RESEA participants alongside the groups on this page.

  • UI claim history for Adult enrollees with a layoff pattern

    A match to UI records could show how many filed claims and how many might have qualified for DW on work history.

  • Claimant take-up by age and education

    California Policy Lab's linked UI and WIOA records could show what share of claimants enroll in Title I within six or twelve months, and through which program.

  • Time from layoff to first service

    Days from dislocation to first staff-assisted service, along the lines of the "days from layoff" measure one local area already tracks.

  • Service-by-service records

    The dates and types of each service within an enrollment would show where people pause and where they step away.

  • Contacts that never become enrollments

    AJCC visits and CalJOBS registrations that never lead to a Title I enrollment would show who reaches the front door and turns back.

  • What the CalJOBS participant summary counts

    The WIOA Participant Summary report for July 2024 to June 2025 shows about a third as many participants as the PIRL file. Knowing which participants it includes would show whether its UI-status rates hold statewide.

About the data

Sources and methods

PIRL. U.S. DOL ETA WIOA Performance Records Public Use File, PY2025 Q1 release (records through September 30, 2025), California participants. Characteristics use people who exited from July 2024 to June 2025; outcomes use those who exited from July 2023 to June 2024. DOL rounds and randomly adjusts wages in the public file. The file has no UI claimant status or reason for separation, and it leaves out San Benito County, so that profile carries funding and labor market data only. A small number of records carry a statewide code and appear in statewide totals but no local profile.

Layoff pattern. $1,300 or more in UI-covered wages in at least two of the three quarters before the enrollment quarter, and unemployed or under layoff notice at entry. $1,300 is California's minimum high-quarter earnings for a UI claim. Other Adult enrollees are split by employment status at entry; California reports no participants as not in the labor force.

RESEA. U.S. DOL ETA 9128 Reemployment Services and Eligibility Assessment workload report, California, quarters ending September 2024 through June 2026, from DOL's Office of Unemployment Insurance, downloaded November 3, 2026. UI claimant ages come from DOL's monthly Characteristics of the Insured Unemployed, July 2024 through June 2025.

CalJOBS. WIOA Participant Summary, statewide, July 2024 to June 2025, run November 2, 2026. Totals are not yet reconciled with PIRL.

Funding. WIOA Adult and DW formula allocations from EDD's Workforce Services Information Notices and allocation tables; DW-to-Adult transfers from local areas' 80% expenditure reports for program years 2023-24 and 2024-25.

Labor market. EDD Labor Market Information Division LAUS annual averages for 2025 and 2019, and QCEW private-sector employment and average weekly wages for the fourth quarter of 2025, built from counties and, for city-based areas, the county that contains them.

Eligibility. EDD Directive WSD24-04, WIOA Title I Eligibility Technical Assistance Guide (Attachment 1, September 2024), section 6.2, "Profiled and Referred UI Claimants," and 20 CFR 680.130(b)(3).

Integrated Service Delivery history. EDD and CWA, State Framework for Implementing ISD and Learning Labs (September 2007); EDD draft directive LLDD-10 (June 2008); WSD08-2 (August 2008); WSD11-4; CWIB WIA annual reports for PY2008-09 and PY2010-11; CSU Northridge ISD evaluation (Phase I, November 2010); EDD Workforce Services Branch Strategic Business Plan 2012-2015; DOL WIASRD public use files.

Exit timing. 20 CFR 677.150(c) and U.S. DOL ETA TEGL 10-16 on the date of program exit.

Emotional modes. DOL ETA and IDEO, "Serving Job Seekers: Elevating the System of Support," workshop materials, April 2013.
