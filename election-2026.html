Election 2026 · Unofficial results as of November 11

What the 2026 election changes for local workforce boards

Voters chose a new governor and new majorities in both houses of Congress. California counts ballots for weeks, and nothing is official until the Secretary of State certifies results on December 11. This page shows where things stand and maps every congressional and legislative district to the local workforce areas inside it.

What stood out

The results that surprised observers

Several longtime incumbents lost or are trailing, and late-counted ballots have changed the lead in close races. Races marked “not called” can still change before certification. Counts are from the Secretary of State’s report of November 11 at 5:17 p.m.

Congress

Called

At least eight Senate seats changed party

Democrats picked up seats in Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Mississippi, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas. Alaska is still being counted.

Called

Mississippi elected a Democrat to the Senate

The AP called the race on November 11 for Scott Colom, a local prosecutor, over Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, a member of the Senate Appropriations Committee. The call came after several days of counting absentee and provisional ballots. Colom will be Mississippi’s first Black senator since Reconstruction.

Called

Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins lost her seat

Troy Jackson defeated Collins in Maine, ending nearly 30 years in the Senate. The committee that writes the Labor-HHS-Education funding bill will have new leaders on both sides.

Announced

Sen. Rand Paul will be the top Republican on Senate HELP

Paul (R-Ky.) said on November 11 that he will become ranking member of the committee that writes WIOA, where Sen. Bernie Sanders is expected to chair. His statement says decisions on health care, education and retirement should rest with families, and it does not mention job training or workforce programs.

Too close to call

Former Education and Workforce Chair Virginia Foxx trails by 335 votes

Rep. Virginia Foxx (R-N.C.), who chaired the committee that writes WIOA in two Congresses, most recently from 2023 to 2025, trails Democrat Chuck Hubbard by 335 votes after counties counted provisional and cured ballots on November 10. She trailed by 99 votes after the initial count. The race has not been called, and results are not official until counties finish their canvass on November 13. If the margin is then 1 percent or less, the trailing candidate can request a recount by noon on November 17.

Leadership

House Democrats elect their leaders November 18 and 19. Rep. Pete Aguilar of California is running for Majority Whip. CD-33

The lame-duck session

Current federal funding expires December 11, and federal workforce programs run at FY 2026 levels until then. Congress can finish full-year FY 2027 bills or extend current funding again. The House committee’s FY 2027 Labor-HHS-Education bill, approved in June, is 2.9 percent below FY 2026 overall.

WIOA reauthorization

A Stronger Workforce for America Act of 2026 cleared the House Education and Workforce Committee this spring, but the full House has not voted. Bills that do not pass by the end of this Congress expire, so reauthorization starts over in January under new committee leadership.

Sacramento

A new governor and a Legislature with more than two dozen new members

The Legislature convenes December 7. The Speaker and Senate leadership announce committee assignments in December or January, and Governor-elect Becerra’s first budget follows on January 10. Becerra launched his transition, titled “California Dreams, California Delivers,” on November 10. Liz Saldivar, a longtime aide, is director of transition, with Arsenio Mataka as policy director, Nicole Elliott as agency review director, and Courtni Pugh and Kenneth E. Maxey II as co-directors of external affairs. The announcement named no one with a labor or workforce portfolio. On November 11 he added two budget advisers: Erika Li, Governor Newsom’s chief deputy director for budget, as transition budget director, and Michael Cohen, Governor Brown’s finance director from 2013 to 2018, as senior adviser for budget.

Key dates

What happens next

  1. Nov. 10
    Mail ballot arrival deadline. Ballots postmarked by Election Day count if they arrived by this date, and counties are still processing them.
  2. Nov. 18–19
    House Democratic leadership elections. The Education and Workforce chair decision is expected in December. Alaska’s ranked-choice tabulation, which is likely to decide its Senate race, is set for November 18.
  3. Dec. 3
    Counties finish their official canvass.
  4. Dec. 7
    Legislature convenes and new members are sworn in.
  5. Dec. 11
    Secretary of State certifies results. Current federal funding expires the same day.
  6. Jan. 1
    H.R. 1 Medi-Cal work requirements take effect in California. The CalFresh work requirements under the same law have applied since June 1, 2026.
  7. Jan. 3
    The 120th Congress convenes.
  8. Jan. 4
    Governor Becerra takes office.
  9. Jan. 10
    Governor’s budget due.
How CWA is engaging

Building relationships with new leaders in both capitals

As races are called, CWA is reaching out to new and returning members of Congress and the Legislature with a short briefing on the local workforce boards in their districts. CWA is also connecting with the offices expected to lead the committees that write and fund WIOA, and with Governor-elect Becerra’s team.

Contact CWA →
  • Tell us who you know. If your organization already works with a new member, let CWA know so we can build on that relationship.
  • Invite a new member to see the work. A visit to an America’s Job Center of California or a training program in their first months in office shows how the system works in their community. CWA can help with timing and talking points.
  • Watch for certified results. CWA will update representation for every local area after December 11.
